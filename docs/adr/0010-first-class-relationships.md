# Relationships become first-class typed edges with a closed, versioned vocabulary

Relations between Entities stop being an untyped single-parent pointer and become **Relationships**: first-class directed edges with their own identity, type, dates, per-type metadata, and their own *published definition*. The vocabulary is **closed at thirteen types** — twelve **record tier** and one **assessment tier** — and versioned as `EDGE_VOCABULARY_VERSION` `1.0.0`. A type outside the vocabulary is a validation violation (`unknown-type`), not an extension point.

Direction is fixed by a naming rule rather than per-type convention: every type reads as "A *type* B", so `fromId` is always A. **No type is symmetric** — there is no edge whose two endpoints are interchangeable, so no reader has to query both directions and no writer has to normalise endpoint order.

This ADR **supersedes ADR [0004](0004-entity-profile-tagged-union.md) on two points** and leaves the rest of it standing.

- 0004 made parent/child **Hierarchy** *the* core relation of any Entity ("a corporate control chain and a shipowner chain are also trees"). Half of that is right — hierarchy is generic, not military — and half does not survive contact with the adjacent domains: those chains are trees only when you look at one relation at a time. Hierarchy becomes **one derived view** over typed edges (active organic `subordinate_to`, plus `corporate_parent`), and `Entity.parentId` is retained as a derived, non-authoritative convenience field rather than the record.
- 0004 fixed the generalisation as "G2-strict": no profile beyond Unit — and later Corporate — would be authored. The vocabulary has to *name* the kinds its edges connect, so `vessel`, `person` and `equipment_class` are authored now as **field-less** profiles carrying only their `kind` discriminant. Every field-bearing profile decision 0004 deferred stays deferred, including the `Entity` field mirror.

What 0004 decided about the *shape* of a profile — a flat tagged union, fields at the top level, never an open attribute bag — is unchanged, and is precisely why three new kinds cost nothing to add here.

## Why

Three separate pressures land on the same missing concept.

**The domains Gabriel is generalising into are not trees.** A supply chain, a shareholding, a shipment, a chartering arrangement and an insurance cover are many-to-many, time-bounded, and frequently several at once between the same pair of entities. `parentId` expresses exactly one relation per entity, undated, untyped and unattributed. Every one of those domains would otherwise arrive as a bespoke side-table, which is the fragmentation ADR 0004 generalised the Entity to avoid.

**A relation is a publishable claim, so it needs a published definition.** Gabriel's output is a CC-BY dataset. A reuser who reads `owned_by` in an export reconstructs its meaning from the type name unless we fix that meaning ourselves, so every type carries a `publicDefinition` written as publishable prose and shipped **verbatim** alongside the data — not a developer comment, not a paraphrase of the PRD. Several definitions exist to make a *negative* statement, which is the whole reason they are authored rather than generated: `corporate_parent` states that where no percentage is recorded, no ownership share, controlling interest or acquisition date has been established, and that the edge is not on its own a statement of legal control.

**Publication risk varies by type, which makes the type the right place for the export gate to act.** This is why `corporate_parent` and `owned_by` are split rather than collapsed into one ownership edge with a percentage. `corporate_parent` is organisation-to-organisation corporate structure — a structural fact about companies. `owned_by` names a **natural person** holding an entity, and is gated out of the public dataset by the natural-person clause. Collapsed, the gate would have to re-derive that distinction from the endpoint's `kind` at publication time, which is both the latest and the worst moment to discover that a row names an individual. Split, the risk is legible in the type name in every picker, every validation message and every export rule. The two types therefore have deliberately disjoint `toKinds` (`corporate` versus `person`), and a test asserts it, because re-collapsing them is the exact mistake the split exists to prevent.

**The assessment tier exists because the most investigatively valuable relations are the ones nobody filed.** "This company is the sole state intermediary, therefore it acts for that ministry" is a judgement Gabriel's analysts make from documentary material; it is not itself documentary. Dropping such edges loses the finding. Publishing them beside registry filings launders judgement into record. So the tier is a partition of the vocabulary, and the caveat is carried **in the type's own `publicDefinition`** — `acts_for` opens with "ASSESSMENT — not a documentary record" and closes by asking the reader to weigh it as an analytical judgement. The label has to live in the definition rather than in the UI because the definition is the only part of it that survives export into somebody else's tooling.

**`ExportOverride` is per-edge and sits outside `RelationshipMetadata`** for two independent reasons. Structurally, every `RelationshipMetadata` key is owned by exactly one declaring type and a key on any other type is an `invalid-metadata` violation; export authorisation is cross-type by nature and would be the one exception that dissolves that invariant. Substantively, authorising publication is a judgement about *one* assessed edge and its evidence — a per-type or per-project switch would authorise edges nobody has read. It records a proposer, a different confirmer, a date and a rationale. That two-person rule enforces **ceremony and attribution, not authentication**: Gabriel has no identity system, the two names are free text, and git history supplies the real attribution. Absence means exclusion, so the gate fails closed by default rather than by configuration.

**Closing and versioning the vocabulary** is what makes the rest enforceable: a closed set means the validator can reject an unknown type, the target picker is finite, and a downstream reuser can code against a stable schema. `EDGE_VOCABULARY_VERSION` plus a test that locks the count at thirteen makes amendment a deliberate act — edit the vocabulary and the test together, and bump the version.

## Considered options

- **Keep `parentId` and add per-domain side-tables when each domain lands.** Rejected: it re-implements dates, provenance and validation per domain, and puts the publication decision in whichever module happens to own the table.
- **A free-text or open `type: string` edge.** Rejected: nothing to publish a definition against, nothing for the validator to reject, and no partition for the export gate. It also silently absorbs typos as new relation types.
- **One tier, with a numeric confidence field instead of an assessment tier.** Rejected: a confidence scalar is something an exporter has to *interpret*, and the interpretation would sit in the exporter. A tier is a partition the gate can act on without judgement. Credibility already exists per-Claim (ADRs [0006](0006-source-claim-first-class-model.md), [0009](0009-machine-never-confirms.md)); reusing it here would conflate "how believable is this record" with "is this a record at all".
- **Collapse `corporate_parent` and `owned_by` into one ownership edge with a `percent`.** Rejected on publication risk (above). Cheaper to model, and it moves the natural-person question to the worst possible moment.
- **Put `exportOverride` in `RelationshipMetadata`.** Rejected: it breaks the one-declaring-type invariant that makes metadata validation possible at all.
- **Allow symmetric types (`affiliated_with`, `related_to`).** Rejected: a symmetric type makes every query a two-direction query and every write a normalisation decision, forever, in exchange for saving one word in a type name.
- **Defer `corporate_parent` to the migration slice that needs it.** Rejected: adding to the *initial* list is authoring; adding after `1.0.0` ships is an amendment that costs a version bump. It costs nothing now and a version later.

## Consequences

- **The vocabulary ships with no runtime consumer, and this is the known gap.** The first slice delivers the types, the thirteen definitions and the validator — and nothing reads them. There is no `relationships` table, no migration, no store field, no UI, no export gate and no `Claim.relationshipId`. The vocabulary is therefore authored against the spec rather than against real data, and the first honest test of it is the migration slice, which runs against the real project file rather than a fixture. Sequencing is in [`docs/timelines/GABRIEL_V2_SLICE_0_1_BUILD.md`](../timelines/GABRIEL_V2_SLICE_0_1_BUILD.md).
- **The thirteen `publicDefinition` strings are a published artefact, not source comments.** Changing one is a change to the dataset, reviewed as prose and versioned with `EDGE_VOCABULARY_VERSION`, not a refactor.
- **`fromKinds` / `toKinds` are advisory.** They order and filter the target picker and never reject an edge. Endpoint validation is existence-based (`dangling-endpoint`): a missing entity is a bug, whereas a surprising kind is usually a modelling opinion and sometimes the finding.
- **Widening `EntityKind` breaks a decoder that still typechecks.** The persisted `kind` decoder returned `"unit"` for every value that was not `"corporate"`, and the narrower return type stays assignable after the union widens — so a `vessel` row would round-trip back as a unit with no compiler error. It becomes an allowlist check against `ENTITY_KINDS` in the same slice that widens the union.
- **`Entity.parentId` is kept, derived, and is not a backup.** If `relationships` is ever empty the derived parent goes null and the persisted column is nulled in the same save; primary and "backup" fail together, perfectly correlated. The backup is the private repository's git history, and the pre-migration commit is pinned before the migration first runs.
- **`corporate_parent` is hierarchy-bearing.** It must sit alongside active organic `subordinate_to` in the derived parent map, because the existing corporate links *are* the industry hierarchy as it stands; omitting it drops those entities out of their tree on the first save.
- **Fail-closed export, enforced by the validator.** An assessment-tier edge without an `ExportOverride` is excluded; a structurally malformed override decodes to "no override" rather than throwing; and an override on a **record-tier** edge is an `invalid-export-override` violation, because there it authorises nothing and reads as if it did.
